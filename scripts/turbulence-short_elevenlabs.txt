Your plane suddenly drops. Drinks jump. Your stomach lurches. <break time="0.6s" /> Here's what's really going on.

Turbulence is just air moving at different speeds, like a boat crossing choppy water. Rising heat, mountains, storms, and those fast jet streams all stir it up.

<break time="0.5s" />

And your plane is built for it. Airliners are designed to handle at least two and a half times the force of gravity. Then engineers add a fifty percent safety margin on top.

In one famous test, engineers bent a Boeing seven-seven-seven's wings upward about seven meters before they finally snapped. That's far beyond what turbulence should ever do.

So what actually gets hurt? <break time="0.5s" /> People. Most turbulence injuries happen to people who aren't wearing a seatbelt.

So next time that seatbelt sign dings, buckle up. <break time="0.4s" /> The plane can handle it. Make sure you can too.
